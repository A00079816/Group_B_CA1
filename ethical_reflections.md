# Ethical Reflection & Improvements
## Critical evaluation and solutions

* In today's world the vast majority of people have smartphones in their pockets or bag and, spend a decent amount of time using them for work or personal reasons. 
* Modern phones have high quality cameras that give anyone and everyone the ability record what they see in public and private and in some cases post it online.
* With the rise of social media, privacy has been thrown out the window even when your not in public spaces, some people still breach your privacy by posting images and videos online. This raises the question should there be harsher laws around what people can do with their phones.

### Data privacy concerns surrounding the use of mobile phones.
The more we use our phones the more it tracks us and sends our data to the manufacturer for processing and in some cases to sell, and that is just from being on the phone. The apps we use also track us but are more likely to sell our data. In our modern digital society data privacy and transparency is extremely important, we need to know why service provides are collecting our information and what they intent to do with it. That is why it is important we have law to protect us from excessive data collection such as the EU's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) which in simple terms requires data controllers to lawfully process data and be transparent about that processing, process data for legitimate reasons, collect only what they need to for the processing, keep data accurate, only store the data for as long as necessary, data must be securely collected and the data controller must be responsible for what happens to the data. (Wolford, 2025)

### Bans on the use of smartphones/camera phones.
When mobile phones with digital cameras started to release to the public in late 2000 - early 2001 in Japan and 2002 in the rest of the world we started to cases of businesses ban the use of them after they were being used mischievously in places such as gyms where strict bans were enforced to prevent client embarrassment of being photographed in changing rooms on equipment (Dunphy, Prendergast and ScolaÍ, 2014).
Today we still see bans on smartphones in places such as the Dáil who completely prohibits the use of smartphone by TDs. In Ireland (and many other countries) there is a ban on the use of mobile phones in primary schools

### How the switch improved peoples everyday lives.
Smartphones have improved our lives in vast ways. We can make calls and text from nearly anywhere (depending on signals). This has kept families and friends connected around the world when they go on holidays or move away. 
The ability for mobile phones to show us our exact location using GPS has made traveling to place we haven't been to before easier and, has made trips to places you know more efficient by giving you the fastest routes while avoiding traffic. It could also be said that it has saved lives of hikers by showing their last know position if they got lost.
Being able to take pictures instantly using a smartphone has made it easier to create memories with family and friends that will last a lifetime (if correctly backed up).
