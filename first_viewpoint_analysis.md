# 1. First Viewpoint Analysis: Social Impact of Mobile Devices

## Introduction: How Phones Changed Society (1980–2009)
The introduction of smart devices has revolutionized the way people communicate with one another and has had a massive impact on society. This section discusses the evolution of mobile devices to smart devices from Nokia, and how it reshaped culture, education, and global communication.  

## Key Social Shifts

### 1. Transformation of Communication
*   **From Voice to Text:** The introduction of SMS in the 1990s created a new "language" (text speak) and allowed for asynchronous communication, changing how generations interacted.
*   **Always-On Culture:** By the late 2000s, the expectation of immediate response began to blur the lines between private time and social availability.

### 2. Impact on Education
Much like other career sectors, education has sought many major improvements due to mobile devices being accessible to students and teachers. Schools now integrating the internet and computer access has allowed for improved accessibility to information where in the past, gathering information was restricted to books and by location. One such impact smart phones have provided is breaking that restriction of location. Smart devices offer education to students through distant learning without restricting them to classrooms, while delivering quality education (Sarwar and Rahim Soomro, 2013). This advancement can allow teachers to deliver education to students facing absences due to problems with health or illness. With improved collaboration using smart devices can prevent students from falling behind (Sarwar and Rahim Soomro, 2013).    

Despite the many benefits, education with smart devices can deliver to institutional systems. Many schools have reported issues of the misuse of mobile devices in school, such as; device usage for malicious purposes in exam settings, phones distracting students during lectures, devices used to disrupt the learning of other students, etc. Such usage allows for students to illegally access the answers or utilizing AI, granting these students an unfair advantage during an examination (Ataro, Madara and Simiuy Sitati, 2016).	

### 3. Cultural and Global Connection
The growing use of mobile devices since the 1980's has brought about improved global communication, especially for countries with a largely migrant society and culture. For many families living in these countries, working abroad grants better career opportunities though at the social cost of communication with their families (Madianou and Miller, 2011). With this already prevalent culture in countries such as the Philippines, families have found the use of mobile phones, and internet access integral to remaining in contacting or parenting from long distances (Madianou and Miller, 2011).   

*   **Social Movements:** By the late 2000s, mobile devices began to play a role in organizing social movements and spreading awareness globally.

## Timeline of Social Evolution (1980–2009)

The table below illustrates how technological milestones directly correlated with changes in social behavior:

| Era | Technological Milestone | Social Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1980s** | Early Mobile Phones (e.g., Nokia) | Communication was a luxury; phones were status symbols for elites. |
| **1990s** | SMS & GSM Networks | Texting emerged as a youth culture phenomenon; privacy increased. |
| **2000s** | Camera Phones & 3G | Visual sharing began; early mobile internet connected people globally. |
| **2007-2009** | Smartphones (iPhone/Android) | The "Always-On" society; social media moved to pockets; constant connectivity. |

## Global Perspectives
The social impact varied by region. In developed nations, phones became extensions of identity. In developing nations, they often served as critical tools for community organization and accessing basic services, leapfrogging traditional infrastructure.
